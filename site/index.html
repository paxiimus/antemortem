ante mortem, adj. Made before death. Published after.

Last words, sealed until you're gone.

Write the note you could never post while you were alive. Ante Mortem keeps it encrypted on your phone. While you keep checking in, nobody can read it, including us. When you stop, it is published to a public cemetery where people can leave condolences.

How it works

Four steps. One of them is a tap.

  1. 01

    Write

    Your public note and your private vault are encrypted on your phone before they leave it. The server stores ciphertext, your email and some dates.

  2. 02

    Check in

    One tap every 30 to 365 days, your choice. Miss it and you get a reminder on the due date, another a week later and a third a week after that.

  3. 03

    Your people confirm

    Thirty days past due, your contacts are asked whether anyone has heard from you. All of them must confirm. A 14-day cooling-off follows, and one check-in from you cancels the release.

  4. 04

    It's published

    The note appears on a public memorial page and your contacts can open the private vault. A time lock on the drand network also opens the note at a date you keep pushing back, so it comes out even if your contacts never act.

The cemetery

A public place for what people left behind.

Every released note gets a memorial page. Anyone can read it and leave a condolence. The page carries no scripts, no tracking and no ads.

Read this before you trust it

What it can't do.

  • Nobody has audited the code. One person wrote and reviewed it.
  • Lose your vault passphrase and the vault is gone. There is no reset, by design.
  • A note is not a will. It has no legal effect.
  • If you stop checking in by accident, the note can still be published. Tell your contacts, and set an interval longer than your longest holiday.
  • The time lock depends on the drand network staying online.
